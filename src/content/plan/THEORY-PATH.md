# The Theory Path: syllabus

The Theory Path is Sixstring Academy's bite-sized theory course: 280 lessons of 2 to 5 minutes in
43 units and three stages, from "what is a note" to augmented sixth chords, always tied to the
guitar neck. This page is the human overview. The source of truth is
[`theory-path.json`](./theory-path.json); [`theory-path.test.ts`](./theory-path.test.ts) checks it.

Each unit will be written by one author from its JSON entries. An entry fixes the idea of each
lesson, the facts to remember, what to show on the neck, the exercises and the drills, so two
authors working from the same entry should produce the same lesson.

## Design principles

1. **Playable knowledge first.** The fourth lesson already ends with a tune ("Hot Cross Buns" on
   string 1, frets 4 2 0). By unit 8 a beginner knows the string names, has tuned with a tuner,
   reads tab, has a first scale under the fingers (A minor pentatonic, box 1) and knows why a chord
   is major or minor (the third: E/Em, A/Am, D/Dm). Standard notation waits until unit 13, and
   options a beginner does not need yet (alternate tunings, exotic scales) wait for later stages.
2. **One idea per lesson.** Each lesson's `teaches` is one sentence a learner could repeat. Lessons
   take 2 to 5 minutes, carry 2 to 5 facts (these become spaced-repetition cards) and have at least
   three exercises.
3. **Always on the neck.** Every lesson says which strings, frets and shapes to show and which figure
   (fretboard, chord box, staff, keyboard, circle of fifths, rhythm line, tab, audio). Theory is never
   left as abstract letters.
4. **Spiral, not blocks.** Rhythm comes back in five places (pulse and beat, strumming, triplets and
   swing, compound and odd meters, plus rhythm on the staff), reading in four (tab, staff, first
   position, up the neck), and intervals twice. They sit between harmony units, not in one long run.
5. **Ear training inside every topic.** There is no separate ear unit. Every unit, and 151 of the
   280 lessons, has a `listen-choice` exercise or an ear drill (`interval-ear`, `chord-quality-ear`,
   `scale-ear`, `cadence-ear`, `rhythm-ear`, `higher-lower`), so learners hear an idea while they
   learn it. Dedicated listening lessons sit in the units where hearing matters most (hearing major
   and minor, intervals by ear, hearing cadences, sevenths, modes and secondary dominants).
6. **Endless practice where the engine can generate it.** `generators` name only drills that exist
   in `src/lessons/generators/params.ts`. Authors set each drill's pools to what has been taught so far
   (for example `scale-notes` with only `major` and tonics C, G, D, F in the major-scale unit).
7. **Prerequisites are knowledge, not just order.** Each lesson lists the lesson before it in its
   unit plus any earlier lesson whose idea it uses (for example, the blues dominant-seventh lesson
   is a prerequisite of "Why V pulls home"). The list is a graph with no cycles, and every
   prerequisite comes earlier in the course.
8. **Correct to a strict teacher.** Spellings follow the key or chord (F# major has E#, Cdim7 is
   C Eb Gb Bbb, E7#9 has F##). Claims that are not always true say "often" or "usually". Guitar is
   written an octave above where it sounds: data and facts give sounding pitch (open strings E2 A2
   D3 G3 B3 E4) and say where each note is written. Roman numerals follow the app's convention
   (A minor is i ii° bIII iv v bVI bVII), and the lesson that introduces them says so and mentions
   the classical III VI VII. Chord shapes are spelled as six fret numbers from string 6 to string 1
   (`x32010`, or `x-10-9-7-8-7` above fret 9), which `major-and-minor-chords/what-a-chord-is`
   teaches before any other lesson relies on it.
9. **Copyright-safe.** Melodies come only from traditional or public-domain music ("Twinkle
   Twinkle", "Frere Jacques", "When the Saints", "My Bonnie", Wagner's Bridal Chorus and Beethoven's
   "Ode to Joy" for interval references, tab reading and chord melody). Everything else is an
   original phrase written for the lesson. No modern song is transcribed or quoted.
10. **Plain words.** A term is defined in the lesson where it first appears; later lessons may use
    it. Notes are written in ASCII (F#, Bb, F##), and display formatting is left to the theory
    module.

## Writing a lesson from an entry

- `Lesson.id` = the slug after the slash (slugs are unique across the course), `skill` =
  `theory:<unit-id>:<lesson-slug>`, `requires` = the prerequisite slugs, `minutes` as given.
- `teaches` becomes the lesson's `teaches`. Open with a teaching card that shows the `onTheGuitar`
  figure, then the exercises in order, then a generated drill if any. Each fact becomes a `Fact`
  (front: a question, back: the fact), with an `ask` exercise where one fits.
- The answers in brackets in `exercises[].idea` are the expected answers; derive and test them with
  `@/core/theory` and `@/core/guitar` rather than retyping them.
- Exercises with a microphone (`play-note`, `play-chord`, `play-phrase`) use the engine's
  non-microphone fallback; nothing else is needed.
- `checkpoint` lists what the unit's test-out quiz must cover; build it as authored `steps` or a
  `sample` drawn from those lessons.
- Text marked "original" must be written for the app. Do not swap in a modern song.

## JSON shape

```json
{ "course": { "id": "theory-path", "title": "...", "stages": [
  { "id": "beginner | intermediate | expert", "title": "...", "units": [
    { "id": "kebab-case, unique", "title": "...", "summary": "one sentence",
      "whyItMatters": "one sentence a learner cares about",
      "lessons": [
        { "id": "unit-id/lesson-slug, unique", "title": "...",
          "teaches": "the one idea, one sentence",
          "prerequisites": ["lesson ids, all earlier in the course"],
          "facts": ["2 to 5 atomic, checkable statements (spaced-repetition cards)"],
          "onTheGuitar": "strings, frets, shapes and the figure to show",
          "exercises": [ { "type": "choice | true-false | match-pairs | order | fill-blanks | type-answer | listen-choice | tap-rhythm | fretboard-find | fretboard-name | chord-build | staff-read | staff-place | play-note | play-chord | play-phrase | flashcard", "idea": "what is asked, with the answer in brackets" } ],
          "generators": ["drill names from src/lessons/generators/params.ts"],
          "minutes": 3 } ],
      "checkpoint": "what a test-out quiz for this unit must cover" } ] } ] } }
```

## Checks (`npx vitest run src/content/plan/theory-path.test.ts`)

- **Structure:** three stages in order; 35 to 45 units of 5 to 8 lessons; 200 to 280 lessons; unique
  slug ids (lesson slugs unique on their own too); exactly the fields above; 2 to 5 distinct facts;
  at least 3 exercises of allowed types; known generators; integer minutes from 2 to 5; no
  placeholders; no Unicode accidentals.
- **Prerequisites:** every one exists and comes earlier, every lesson after the first has at least
  one, and there are no cycles.
- **Engine cross-checks:** the scale and chord spellings quoted in facts; the diatonic chords and
  numerals of C, G and A minor, and the seventh chords of A and E minor (with V7, vii°7 and
  AmMaj7 from harmonic minor); the secondary dominants and borrowed chords of C; key-signature
  counts; interval sizes; the five pentatonic boxes, fifth-position notes and octave shapes; the
  five CAGED positions of G major and its three-notes-per-string pattern; the keys said to lie in
  one position; the inlay frets; every triad shape on three strings; the drop-2 voicings; **every
  six-string chord shape written in the text** (each sounded note must belong to the chord, with
  the right bass note); the frets of the traditional tunes, the pickup and the non-chord-tone
  examples; staff positions of the open strings and ledger-line notes; open-string frequencies,
  fret spacing, cents, harmonics and the error of tuning by harmonics; meters, time signatures,
  common and cut time, the dotted eighth, swing, tempo words and the capo example.

## Id changes after the expert review

Other files that refer to lesson ids should follow these changes. No id was renamed or reused.

- **Added:** `major-and-minor-chords/reading-a-chord-box` (split out of `what-a-chord-is`, which
  now teaches the chord, its root and the `x32010` spelling), `strumming-rhythm/pickup-notes`,
  `caged/major-scale-positions`, `seventh-chords/sevenths-in-a-minor-key`,
  `voice-leading/non-chord-tones`.
- **Deleted:** `the-six-strings/what-a-tuning-is` (standard tuning is now defined in
  `the-six-strings/string-names`; drop D, DADGAD and open G stay in
  `tuning-and-physics/alternate-tunings`), `capo-and-transposition/transposing-with-numerals`
  (folded into `capo-and-transposition/transposing`). A reference to either should point to the
  lesson named in brackets.
